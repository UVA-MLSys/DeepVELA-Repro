# Contract and planning

Create a finite `campaign-contract`, `target-manifest`,
`benchmark-assessment`, and `verification-policy` before full execution.

## Campaign contract

Record the objective, intended claim, target scope, permissions, resource and
retry budgets, permitted terminal dispositions, stopping condition, and human
decision boundaries. A prompt establishes requested scope and choices; it is
not authoritative evidence for scientific facts.

## Target manifest

Each target is one unit for which an evaluator can produce a terminal result.
Record its exact key, result grain, reference availability, and required
obligation IDs. Avoid vague cohorts such as "all normal cases."

## Readiness assessment

Score and cite the six ontology categories separately. Use assessment gaps to
create unresolved obligations. Readiness describes the benchmark specification;
it does not accept or reject a reproduction result.

## Verification policy

Freeze the comparator, join keys, result grain, replicate and seed design,
aggregation, uncertainty procedure, tolerance or equivalence boundary,
missing-result behavior, and allowed dispositions before viewing the complete
result cohort. Human-selected tolerances must be identified as human decisions.

Record the scientific rationale and authoritative reference sources for this
policy. The agent may not tune a comparator after seeing reproduced values.
Represent closure as executable predicates over target dispositions, graph
validity, active discrepancies, invalidations, and obligation checks; retain a
description, but do not use prose alone as the stopping gate.

## Action planning

Each action declares its bindings, obligations, inputs, expected artifacts,
external effects, retry limit, and resource estimate. Order preflight from low
to high cost. Do not launch an action whose scientific meaning or external
effects depend on an unresolved decision.
